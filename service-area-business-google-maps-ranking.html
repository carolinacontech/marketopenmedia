Can a Service-Area Business Rank on Google Maps Without Showing an Address?

Market Open Media · Updated September 24, 2026

Most contractors work out of a home or a shop they don't want customers showing up at. Here's what Google actually allows.

Yes. Google explicitly supports "service-area businesses" — if you don't serve customers at your business address, Google's own guidance says to leave the address field blank in your Business Profile. Google still requires an address behind the scenes for verification, but it's never shown publicly.

What Google's Policy Actually Says

Google's guidance is direct: if you don't serve customers at your business address, don't enter one under the "Info" tab, and leave the business location field blank. That's the official path for most contractors — tree service, roofing, plumbing, HVAC — who work at the customer's property, not a storefront.

Your service areas (the cities and neighborhoods you cover) are set separately from your address, and that's what determines where your business can show up in local search.

Does Hiding Your Address Hurt Ranking?

This is where it gets genuinely unsettled. Google hasn't published data confirming or denying a ranking impact from hiding your address. In the local SEO industry, you'll find both positions argued: some practitioners report no measurable difference, others report a visibility drop after hiding an address. We're not going to tell you one side has it right when Google hasn't said so — what we will say is that your service-area settings, review activity, and profile completeness matter more than whether the address line is visible.

What This Means in Practice

If you don't want your home or shop address public, Google's own rules let you hide it — that's not a workaround, it's how service-area businesses are meant to be set up. Get the service-area boundaries right, keep your profile complete, and don't lose sleep over whether the address line itself is costing you rankings; there's no solid evidence either way.
