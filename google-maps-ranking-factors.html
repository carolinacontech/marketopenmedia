Google Maps Ranking Factors: What Actually Moves the Map Pack

Market Open Media · Updated September 24, 2026

Google publishes its local ranking factors directly. Here's what they actually say, in plain terms, for a contractor trying to move up the Map Pack.

Google ranks local results on three factors: relevance (how well your profile matches the search), distance (how close you are to the searcher), and prominence (how well-known your business is, based largely on reviews). There's no official percentage breakdown of how much each one counts.

Relevance

Google's own definition: "Relevance is how well a Business Profile matches what someone is searching for." For a contractor, that means your Google Business Profile's category, services list, and description need to actually say what you do — "HVAC repair" and "AC installation" as their own line items, not buried in a generic "local local home services" description.

Distance

Google's definition: "Distance refers to how far each business is from the customer who's searching." If a searcher hasn't shared their location, Google estimates it from what it knows. This is largely outside your control — you can't change where a customer is searching from — but it's why service-area businesses need accurate service-area settings so Google understands which searches you're actually eligible to show up for.

Prominence

Google's definition: "Prominence means how well-known a business is. Prominent places are more likely to show up in search results." Google explicitly says more reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking, and also factors in things like how many websites link to your business. This is the factor most contractors can move fastest — review volume and recency are within your control in a way distance isn't.

What Google Itself Recommends

  • Verify your business profile
  • Keep your business information current and complete
  • Respond to customer reviews
  • Add photos and videos

Source: Google Business Profile Help — "Tips to improve your local ranking on Google"

What This Means in Practice

No single fix moves all three factors at once. A complete, accurately categorized profile helps relevance. A steady flow of recent, positive reviews helps prominence. Distance you can't change directly, but you can make sure your service areas are configured correctly so you're not competing for searches you were never going to win.
